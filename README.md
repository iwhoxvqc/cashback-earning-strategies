## What Cashback Offers Really Are

A cashback offer is a deal between a retailer and a cashback platform. The retailer agrees to pay a commission to the platform for every paying customer the platform sends its way. The platform then shares most of that commission back with the shopper as cash. You don't pay more at checkout — the cashback comes out of the retailer's affiliate marketing budget, not a markup on your order. The price you see at the merchant's site is the same price anyone else pays.

The mechanic that makes this work is called last-click attribution. When you click through from a cashback platform to a retailer, a tracking cookie or tag is set. When you complete checkout in that same session, the retailer's affiliate network sees the cashback platform as the last referrer and pays them the commission. The platform credits your share as "pending" cashback. Once the retailer's return window closes and the sale is confirmed, that pending cashback becomes withdrawable.

This is also why cashback offers can be fragile: if you click a coupon-aggregator link or let a competing browser extension auto-apply a code between the cashback click-through and checkout, the last-click attribution can flip to a different referrer, and your cashback silently voids. The fix is simple — start at the cashback platform, click through, stay in that tab, and complete checkout without detours.

## How to Stack Cashback Offers with Promo Codes and Cards

The single most useful thing to understand about cashback offers is that they layer. Four common savings layers can apply to the same online order, and each is funded by a different party — so they don't cancel each other out:

1. **Sale price** — funded by the retailer's pricing decision (Black Friday, Prime Day, July 4th, Labor Day, merchant-specific sales).
2. **Promo code** — funded by the retailer's marketing budget.
3. **Cashback** — funded by the retailer's affiliate marketing budget, paid through ShopBack.
4. **Card rewards** — funded by your card issuer's interchange revenue (Chase, Citi, AMEX, Capital One, Bank of America).

A worked example: a $200 jacket on Amazon with a 20% Black Friday sale, a 10% member code from the merchant's ShopBack page, 5% cashback, and a 1.5% rewards card. Sale drops it to $160. Code drops it to $144 paid at checkout. Cashback returns $7.20. Card rewards return $2.16. Total effective spend against the $200 list price: roughly $135 — about a third off, with all four layers intact.

The one thing that breaks the chain is sourcing the promo code from a third-party coupon-aggregator site. Those sites often drop their own affiliate cookie, overwriting ShopBack's tracking. Use codes listed directly on the merchant's ShopBack page — those are merchant-approved and cashback-compatible.

The reliable habit: ShopBack first → safe code from the merchant's ShopBack page → complete checkout in the same session → pay with a US rewards card.
